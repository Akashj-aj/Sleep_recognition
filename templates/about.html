Project Objectives

Sleep quality plays a critical role in human health and cognitive performance. This academic project evaluates whether self-reported lifestyle, demographic, and physiological metrics can accurately categorize individual sleep quality into three distinct classes: Poor, Moderate, and Good.

The project compares five supervised machine-learning algorithms (Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, SVM, and KNN) to determine which pipeline achieves the highest predictive accuracy and balance across categories.

//

Dataset Overview

The study utilizes the 374-record Sleep Health and Lifestyle Dataset. The dataset incorporates 12 input features spanning:

  • Demographics: Age, Gender, Occupation
  • Lifestyle Habits: Hours of Sleep per Day, Physical Activity Level (minutes/day), Perceived Stress Level (1-10 scale), Daily Steps
  • Physiological & Health Measures: Systolic Blood Pressure, Diastolic Blood Pressure, Heart Rate, BMI Category, Sleep Disorder Diagnosis
///

Preprocessing Pipeline

Data transformation is executed using scikit-learn Pipeline and ColumnTransformer modules:

  • Target Categorization: Raw Quality of Sleep scores (1-10) are binned into Poor (≤5), Moderate (6-7), and Good (8-10).
  • Feature Engineering: Blood Pressure strings (e.g. 126/83) are split into separate Systolic_BP and Diastolic_BP numeric features. Missing Sleep Disorder entries are imputed as "None".
  • Scaling & Encoding: Numeric features are median-imputed and scaled with StandardScaler; categorical features are one-hot encoded using OneHotEncoder.
////

Evaluation Strategy

Models are evaluated using 5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation (StratifiedKFold). Stratification ensures that each fold maintains identical target class proportions as the total dataset.

Random Forest emerged as the top-performing classifier, achieving 98.57% mean cross-validation accuracy and a 94.82% Macro F1 score.

!

Project Limitations & Scope

Input Data Dependencies: Categorical variables like BMI Category and Sleep Disorder require pre-existing diagnostic inputs. The pipeline relies on users supplying these categories or selecting default representations.

Class Imbalance: The dataset contains 374 records, with 33 records in the Poor category, 187 in Moderate, and 154 in Good. While Macro F1 accounts for class imbalance during evaluation, larger and more diverse datasets would improve generalizability.

Educational Disclaimer

This application and its underlying machine-learning models are developed solely for academic demonstration and research purposes. The classifications generated by this software are statistical predictions and do not constitute a medical diagnosis, medical opinion, or clinical advice.
